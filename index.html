Before you call anyone

Six things you can fix in ten minutes.

A good share of the calls we get turn out to be one of these. Try them first — if one works, you've saved a service charge. We'd rather you spend the money when you actually need us.

Remote does nothing

Change the battery first — it is the single most common cause. If a fresh battery doesn't help, re-pair the remote using the Learn button on the motor unit; the sequence is printed on the unit or in its manual.

Door closes, then reverses

The two safety sensors near the floor are blocked or knocked out of alignment. Clear the beam, wipe both lenses, and nudge them until both indicator lights are steady rather than blinking.

Power is out, door won't open

Pull the red emergency release cord hanging from the opener rail — it disconnects the door from the motor so you can lift by hand. Only do this with the door fully closed.

Wall button and remote both dead

Check the breaker and make sure the opener is still plugged in — a GFCI outlet in the garage can trip without anyone noticing. Reset it before assuming the opener has failed.

The door is loud

Spray a garage-door lubricant on the rollers, hinges and springs, then run the door twice. Don't use WD-40 — it strips the grease instead of replacing it.

Keypad stopped working

Replace the keypad battery, then reset the PIN from the motor unit. Outdoor keypads fail early in wet winters, and a replacement is inexpensive.

Please don't attempt these

Three repairs that send people to hospital every year.

  • A broken spring. A torsion spring holds enough stored energy to break bones. It needs winding bars and the correct wire size — and both springs get replaced together, not only the broken one.
  • A door off its track. The door can drop. Don't lever it back — leave it where it is and keep cars and people out from under it.
  • A snapped cable. The door is unbalanced and will fall unevenly if you lift it. Leave it closed.
